## 1. Executive Summary & Research Demarcation

In strict accordance with Section 6 of the campaign research charter, **Meta must not be falsely depicted as supplying military hardware or combat cloud systems**. Meta's relationship to the Palestine-Israel crisis is fundamentally distinct from Google, Amazon, or Microsoft:

* Meta does **not** provide tactical military cloud infrastructure like Project Nimbus.
* Meta's role is centered on its position as the **world's dominant social media and communications gatekeeper**.
* The core documented issues involve **systemic censorship of Palestinian speech**, discriminatory enforcement of content moderation algorithms, compliance with Israeli government removal requests, and overbroad application of terrorism-related platform policies.

## 2. Independent Human Rights Investigations

### 2.1 The BSR Independent Human Rights Due Diligence (September 2022)
Following public outcry and a direct recommendation by the independent Meta Oversight Board, Meta commissioned **Business for Social Responsibility (BSR)** to evaluate its content moderation during the May 2021 Gaza-Jerusalem crisis.

* **Key Finding:** BSR concluded that Meta's policies and enforcement *"appear to have had an adverse human rights impact on the rights of Palestinian users to freedom of expression, freedom of assembly, political participation, and non-discrimination."*
* **Root Causes Identified:**
  1. Over-enforcement of Arabic content compared to Hebrew content.
  2. Severe lack of Arabic dialectal nuance in automated AI classifiers.
  3. Under-enforcement of Hebrew-language incitement and hate speech against Arabs.
  4. Disproportionate reliance on secretive government-aligned watchlists.

### 2.2 Human Rights Watch (HRW) Report (December 2023)
*Report Title:* **"Meta’s Broken Promises: Systemic Censorship of Palestine Content on Instagram and Facebook"**

HRW documented over **1,050 cases of digital censorship across 60+ countries** in October and November 2023 alone. The report identified six distinct, systemic suppression mechanisms:
1. **Content Removals:** Deletion of peaceful posts, news reports, poetry, and documented human rights violations.
2. **Account Suspensions & Bans:** Permanent or temporary bans imposed on Palestinian journalists, cartoonists, and civil society groups without transparent appeal processes.
3. **Impairment of Engagement ("Shadowbanning"):** Algorithmic reduction of account visibility, preventing followers from seeing posts or finding profiles via search.
4. **Restriction of Live Features:** Disabling livestream capabilities for accounts broadcasting live ground developments in Gaza and the West Bank.
5. **Restriction of Interaction:** Blocking users from commenting, liking, or sharing Palestinian solidarity material.
6. **Erroneous Automated Translation:** In October 2023, Instagram auto-translated Palestinian bios containing the Arabic phrase *"Alhamdulillah"* (Praise be to God) and the Palestinian flag emoji as *"Palestinian terrorists are fighting for their freedom"*. Meta apologized, calling it an "algorithmic bug."

### 2.3 7amleh (Arab Center for Social Media Advancement) Research (2021–2026)
A comprehensive joint study with Utrecht University documented over **3,500 verified digital rights violations**, concluding that the cumulative impact of Meta's enforcement constitutes a form of digital erasure (*platformicide*) of Palestinian perspectives.

## 3. Platform Policies & Structural Flaws

### 3.1 Dangerous Organizations and Individuals (DOI) Policy
* Meta's DOI policy heavily incorporates the United States Foreign Terrorist Organization (FTO) list.
* Because Palestinian political leadership and various factional entities in Gaza are designated under US law, Meta strictly suppresses any mention, imagery, or reporting of these entities, even when shared by journalists, neutral monitors, or civilians reporting on bombings.
* The policy disproportionately penalizes Arabic political discourse while giving wide latitude to state militaries and official government spokespersons.

### 3.2 The "Shaheed" Ruling by the Oversight Board (July 2024)
* Meta previously banned all uses of the common Arabic word **"Shaheed"** (martyr) under its DOI policy, leading to more content deletions than almost any other term on the platform.
* In July 2024, the Meta Oversight Board issued a binding ruling stating that this blanket policy was **unnecessary, disproportionate, and unfairly silenced millions of Muslims and Arabic speakers** who use the term benignly in religious, historical, and cultural contexts. Meta subsequently agreed to revise its policy.

### 3.3 "From the River to the Sea" (September 2024)
* In September 2024, the Oversight Board reviewed three cases concerning the slogan *"From the river to the sea, Palestine will be free."*
* The Board concluded that the slogan alone does not constitute hate speech or incitement to violence, acknowledging it as a legitimate expression of solidarity, political self-determination, and equal rights.

## 4. Government Relations & The Israeli Cyber Unit

### 4.1 Extra-Judicial Takedown Requests
* The **Israeli Cyber Unit** (operating under the State Attorney's Office) submits voluntary takedown requests directly to Meta, TikTok, and X, bypassing formal judicial court orders.
* Independent analyses and Israeli official reports indicate that Meta historically complied with **over 85–90%** of these voluntary governmental takedown requests, effectively outsourcing state censorship to a private corporate platform.

### 4.2 WhatsApp & Metadata Concerns
* While WhatsApp features end-to-end encryption for message contents, investigative reporting by *+972 Magazine* (April 2024) noted that Israeli military intelligence algorithms (such as the *Lavender* target-scoring system) utilized metadata, phone records, and civilian presence in messaging groups as statistical features to assign target ratings.
* **Clarification:** There is **no evidence** that Meta directly provided WhatsApp encryption keys to the IDF; metadata interception and device compromise are typically executed via telecom interception, malware (e.g., Pegasus), or on-device extractions.

## 6. Strategic Relevance to Malaysia

* **Information Sovereignty:** WhatsApp is Malaysia's primary daily communications medium (used by over 90% of connected Malaysians), while Facebook and Instagram dominate public discourse and SME commerce.
* **Censorship Vulnerability:** In 2023 and 2024, Malaysian government officials—including Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim—had official speeches and social posts regarding Palestinian solidarity removed or restricted by Meta, sparking formal protests from the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC).
* **Call for Decentralisation:** This demonstrates that dependence on centralized, foreign-owned platforms leaves Malaysian public discourse and state leaders vulnerable to unilateral Silicon Valley algorithmic censorship.
